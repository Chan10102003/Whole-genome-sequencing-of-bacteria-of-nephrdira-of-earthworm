# bacterial-wgs-earthworm-nephridia
Introduction – Key Points
Earthworms are important members of soil ecosystems and contribute significantly to:
Organic matter decomposition
Nutrient cycling
Maintaining soil fertility
Nephridia are specialized excretory organs in earthworms that help in:
Waste removal
Osmoregulation
Earthworm nephridia contain a diverse population of microorganisms, particularly bacteria that may have symbiotic relationships with the host.
These bacteria may contribute to:
Nitrogen metabolism
Detoxification
Microbial homeostasis
Earthworm health
Soil quality
Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS) enables comprehensive analysis of bacterial genomes, including:
Genetic composition
Metabolic pathways
Functional characteristics
Potential biotechnological applications
Studying bacteria associated with earthworm nephridia can provide insights into:
Bacterial physiological functions
Their role in earthworm health
Ecological significance
Host–microbe interactions
WGS-based characterization may also help explore potential applications in:
Sustainable agriculture
Soil bioremediation
Microbial ecology

RESOURCE OF THE SAMPLE :
Earthworms were bought from an authorized commercial earthworms suppliers engaged in live bait or vermiculture. They were brought to the laboratory in the original packaging with the aim of reducing handling stress. They were then moved to sterile plastic vessels lined with humid soil in order to retain suitable humidity levels. The earthworms were acclimatized under maintained laboratory conditions for a certain number of days in order to help them stabilize prior to bacterial isolation. All visibly unhealthy or inactive worms were not included in the experiment.

OBJECTIVE:
To isolate and culture earthworm nephridial bacterial strains by microbiological and molecular methods.
To isolate and purify high-quality genomic DNA from the cultured bacterial strains for sequencing and downstream applications.
To carry out whole genome sequencing by next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies to achieve complete genetic data.
To assemble and annotate the bacterial genome to determine coding regions, regulatory elements, and functional pathways.
To identify the presence and functionality of genes using bioinformatics methods.
To perform phylogenetic analysis to follow the evolution of bacteria and co-speciation with earthworm hosts.

Eudrilus eugeniae: 
The African Night crawler
Eudrilus eugeniae, or the African night crawler, is an earthworm species indigenous to the tropical and subtropical areas of Africa. It is extensively employed in vermiculture because it has a fast growth rate, a high reproductive rate, and an effective organic waste decomposition capacity. The species can tolerate warm and humid conditions and is therefore applicable for composting and soil enrichment. Eudrilus eugeniae is described by its size, which can go up to 15–25 cm, and has a reddish-brown or purple color. It possesses a well-developed clitellum and is highly mobile relative to other earthworms. Its life cycle is not long, with sexual maturity established in 5–6 weeks, and it gives rise to cocoons with more than one hatchling. Besides being used in composting and soil aeration, Eudrilus eugeniae has also been investigated for its link to other microorganisms, such as bacteria found within its nephridia, which are responsible for nitrogen cycling and organic matter breakdown. Since it can carry helpful microbes, it is also a significant model to investigate microbial diversity in the soil ecosystem. Because of its versatility and effectiveness in the decomposition of organic matter, Eudrilus eugeniae is extensively utilized in vermicomposting, eco-friendly agriculture, and environmental biotechnology.

Earthworm anatomy and dissection:
To reduce external bacterial contamination, the earthworms were rinsed gently with sterile distilled water and then a mild surface sterilization in 70% ethanol. The morphological characteristics were inspected to ascertain the species and only healthy, active earthworms were used for nephridia dissection and bacterial analysis. Upon dissection with the help of a surgical blade and ice plate nephridia (excretory system) were found in the tail region of the earthworm (14-16 segment) where the head region consists of various other organs such as digestive organs like pharynx esophagus, crop, gizzard, intestines as well as reproductive organs such as testes, ovaries, seminal vesicles and the nervous system such as brain, ganglia, and nerve cord. Also, the red thread-like structure in the center was identified which is called the dorsal vesicle which is the earthworm’s circulatory system. The dissection gave a clear idea about the location of nephridia and how to isolate it. Nephridia is present as pairs on either side which is their excretory system. In general, there are three types septal nephridia (segment 15 onwards), integumentary nephridia (segment 7), Pharyngeal Nephridia (segments 4, 5, 6)

Methodology:
Serial dilution:
The bacterial sample was serially diluted from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵ using sterile distilled water.
Spread plating:
Selected dilutions were spread on sterile agar plates using an L-shaped spreader and incubated at 37°C for 24 hours.
Bacterial isolation: 
Three morphologically distinct colonies were selected and purified using the quadrant streaking technique, followed by subculturing for preservation.
Microbial suspension: 
The isolated bacterial cultures were inoculated into nutrient broth and incubated at 37°C for 24 hours to obtain bacterial suspensions.
Gram staining: 
Bacterial smears were prepared and stained using crystal violet, Gram’s iodine, decolorizer, and safranin, followed by microscopic observation at 100× magnification.
DNA extraction:
Bacterial genomic DNA was extracted using cell lysis, washing, phenol-chloroform extraction, RNA removal, alcohol precipitation, and resuspension in TE buffer.
DNA quality assessment: 
DNA purity and integrity were evaluated before sequencing.
Whole-genome sequencing: 
One isolated bacterial culture was selected for Illumina whole-genome sequencing at Gene Aura Genetic Clinic, Chennai.
